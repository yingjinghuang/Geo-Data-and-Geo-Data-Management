Course Project · 30%

Final Project

An open-ended team project centred on a meaningful GeoData problem, decision, or question.

Working project direction

The final project is open-ended in form but GeoData-centred in reasoning. Students are not required to build the same kind of artifact or follow the weekly course sequence.

Build

A small spatial database, structured geodata collection, reusable data package, schema, workflow, or lightweight prototype.

Benchmark / Compare

Compare formats, representations, resolutions, storage strategies, query approaches, data sources, or integration choices.

Investigate

Ask a GeoData research question about how a source, representation, spatial support, resolution, or management choice changes a result.

Evaluate / Extend

Audit quality, interoperability, provenance, reproducibility or reuse, or extend a method, workflow, schema, or management strategy.

Common requirement
There should be a meaningful GeoData problem, decision, or question, and enough evidence to evaluate the proposed answer, solution, comparison, or finding.

Possible outputs

A project may produce a database, dataset, benchmark, workflow, analysis, prototype, documented comparison, empirical finding, or another justified output. The form of the output is secondary to the quality of the GeoData reasoning, execution, evidence, and reflection.

Week 11 presentation

The Week 11 presentation remains part of the final project assessment, but the final project is not “just a presentation.” The presentation is where the team explains and defends the broader project: what it tried to do, which GeoData decisions mattered, what evidence was produced, and what limitations or trade-offs remain.
