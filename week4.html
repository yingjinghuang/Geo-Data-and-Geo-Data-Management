Week 4: Raster Data

How do raster grids store geographic information, and how do we locate them on a map?

Week Content

This week introduces raster data through small NumPy arrays before moving to georeferenced GeoTIFFs and real Vienna datasets.

  • Raster grids, bands, cell size and spatial resolution
  • NoData values, validity masks and raster statistics
  • Origins, affine transforms, CRS, bounds and cell coordinates
  • Reading and writing GeoTIFFs with Rasterio
  • Continuous elevation data and categorical land-cover data

Materials

Lecture slides · PDF Lab notebook · IPYNB Vienna DEM clip · GeoTIFF Vienna WorldCover clip · GeoTIFF

Lab

Parts A and B reinforce the lecture workflow with a synthetic DEM and a real Vienna elevation raster. Keep the notebook and both GeoTIFF files in the same folder when working in JupyterHub.

Part C is the independent WorldCover exercise. Work through the notebook step by step: inspect metadata, build a valid-cell mask, calculate class counts and shares, identify the most common class, create a categorical map, and then write a short interpretation. Use the separate code cell provided for each stage.

Lab Checkpoint

Submit the completed Week 4 notebook to Moodle.

Save the notebook with your outputs visible. Your WorldCover section should include the requested metadata, class statistics, categorical map, and short interpretation.
