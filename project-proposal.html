Project Proposal

Prepare a concise team proposal for an open-ended GeoData project.

Project idea

The final project may build, investigate, compare, evaluate, or extend something around a meaningful GeoData problem, decision, or question. A database, dataset, benchmark, workflow, analysis, prototype, or empirical finding can all be appropriate outcomes.

Proposal scope

Keep the proposal concise (approximately 1–2 pages). At this stage, the proposal is a plan rather than a contract; reasonable changes during Weeks 9–10 are expected when evidence or feasibility changes.

  1. Project objective — what do you want to build, investigate, compare, evaluate, or understand?
  2. Why it is a GeoData project — which GeoData issue, decision, data characteristic, or methodological challenge matters?
  3. Data / resources — likely datasets, sources, formats, tools, and access risks.
  4. Planned work — what will you actually do?
  5. Evidence / validation — how will you evaluate the answer, solution, comparison, or finding?
  6. Expected output — what will the project produce?
  7. Team roles, timeline, and fallback — how will the work be divided and what is the smaller feasible version?
Project principle
Course topics are resources, not a checklist. A strong project may connect several relevant ideas or investigate one or two GeoData issues in greater depth.

The final template, exact deadline, submission location, and detailed rubric wording will be confirmed in class and on Moodle.
